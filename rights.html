Mental Healthcare Act 2017

Nine rights, in plain words.

The Act applies to every clinic and hospital in India, including this one. The section numbers are there so you can quote them.

Right to access mental healthcare

Good-quality, affordable care, close to where you live, without discrimination on the basis of gender, caste, religion, income or anything else. The same applies in a government hospital.

Section 18

Right to confidentiality

What you tell your doctor stays with the people treating you. It is released only with your consent, or when the law requires it, such as a real threat to your life or someone else's. Nothing goes to the media or an employer.

Sections 23 and 24

Right to access your records

You can read your medical record and take a copy. A doctor may withhold one specific part only if reading it would cause you serious harm, and must tell you that something was withheld.

Section 25

Right to community living

You have the right to live in your community, with your family or on your own, with support. Nobody can be kept in a hospital or institution only because a family does not want to take them home.

Section 19

Right to be free from cruel treatment

A clean, safe place, privacy, proper clothing and food. No chaining, ever. No ECT without anaesthesia and a muscle relaxant, and no ECT for children. Seclusion and restraint only to prevent immediate harm, and only as the Act allows.

Sections 20, 95 and 97

Right to information

You must be told what your illness is called, what treatment is proposed, what it is for, its known side effects, and the alternatives, in a language you understand. Including your rights under the Act.

Section 22

Right to make an Advance Directive

While you are well, you can write down how you want to be treated, and not treated, if you ever lose the ability to decide. Doctors must follow it, except in an emergency. Build yours below.

Section 5

Right to a Nominated Representative

You choose one adult who speaks for you if you cannot speak for yourself, and who must be consulted on admission and treatment. It does not have to be your nearest relative. Name yours below.

Section 14

If someone attempts suicide, they are not a criminal

A person who attempts suicide is presumed to be under severe stress and is not tried or punished. The government must provide care. There is no police case to fear, so call for help without hesitating. What to do tonight.

Section 115
Where to complain Every district has a Mental Health Review Board under the Act. If you feel a right has been violated, in this clinic or anywhere else, you or your nominated representative can apply to the Board. Reception will give you the address, and we will not treat you differently for asking.

Section 14 · Nominated Representative

One person who speaks for you when you cannot.

In most Bhavnagar families it is assumed that the father, the husband or the eldest son decides. The Act lets you choose. It can be your spouse, a sibling, a friend, or anyone you trust.

Who can be one

  • Any adult, 18 or older, who is able to make decisions and agrees in writing.
  • A relative, a friend, or a person from a registered organisation that supports people with mental illness.
  • If you name nobody, the Act picks in order: a person named in your Advance Directive, then a relative, then a caregiver, then a person appointed by the Review Board.
  • You can change the nomination at any time while you are able to decide.

What they can do

  • Be told when you are admitted, and be consulted on the treatment plan.
  • Help you make decisions, and make them on your behalf when you cannot, while respecting your Advance Directive.
  • Apply to the Mental Health Review Board on your behalf, including against an admission.
  • Be involved in planning your discharge and your care at home.
  • See the parts of your record needed for your care.

What they cannot do

  • Override a valid Advance Directive.
  • Consent to ECT for a child, or to unmodified ECT for anyone.
  • Keep you admitted when the conditions in the Act are no longer met.
  • Read your therapy notes or share your information outside your care.
  • Decide about your property, marriage or money. The Act covers mental healthcare only.

Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023

What we keep about you, and what we do not.

Written to be read, not scrolled past. The Act gives you consent, purpose limitation and the right to erasure. This is how we honour them.

What we collect

Only what you give us at booking and in the consulting room: a name or alias, a phone number, what is troubling you, and the clinical record your doctor writes. Browsing this site collects nothing; there are no trackers and no analytics cookies. Self-check scores stay in your browser unless you attach them to a booking.

Why we collect it

To treat you, to remind you of visits and medicines, to let a doctor prepare before you walk in, and to keep the record the medical council requires. We do not use your data for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not share it with insurers or employers.

Who sees it

Your doctors, and reception for booking and reminders. A caregiver sees your medicine schedule, appointments and the red-flags guide only after you switch on Family Circle, and never your therapy notes. You can switch it off at any time.

How long we keep it

Clinical records for as long as medical rules require and while you remain our patient. A booking that never becomes a visit is deleted within 90 days. Anonymous Saathi conversations are not stored at all. When you ask for erasure, we delete everything the law does not oblige us to keep, and tell you what remains.

WhatsApp

Reminders, "report ready" messages and prescription copies go on WhatsApp only after you tick the consent box at booking. Reply STOP to any message and they end. We never discuss your illness in a WhatsApp message beyond what you asked for.

ABHA linking is optional

ABHA, the Ayushman Bharat Health Account, is a 14-digit health ID that can link your records across hospitals. We link your record only if you ask, you can unlink later, and refusing changes nothing about your care here.

Your rights over your data

  • Access: see what we hold and get a copy.
  • Correction: fix anything that is wrong.
  • Erasure: ask us to delete what is no longer needed.
  • Withdraw consent for WhatsApp, Family Circle or ABHA at any time.

How we use AI

Seven rules Kiran Saathi cannot break.

Saathi is the clinic's AI companion. These rules are written into the product, not into a policy nobody reads.

  • It never diagnoses, and never names a probable disorder to a patient.
  • It never advises starting, stopping or changing a dose. It reads your prescription back and explains it.
  • Any risk language returns the crisis card first and alerts the clinic. The conversation is not left to the model.
  • It speaks your language, and mirrors Gujarati script or transliteration the way you wrote it.
  • It tells you it is an AI companion from the clinic, not a doctor, at the start of every new session.
  • It stores nothing from anonymous sessions. Consented data is stored under the DPDP Act with a visible audit trail.
  • Every clinical draft, from an intake summary to a prescription, carries its provenance and is a draft until a doctor signs it.

The ambient scribe, and your right to say no

With your permission, the doctor may record the consultation so that software drafts the note and prescription, giving you more of the doctor's attention and less of their keyboard. The rules:

  • Recording happens only after your consent is itself recorded, at the start of the visit.
  • The audio is deleted once the doctor has edited and signed the note.
  • You can say no, or say stop halfway, at any time. Your care does not change in any way.
AI never decides anything about your care. A doctor does. Saathi can remind, explain, listen and prepare. The diagnosis, the prescription and every change to them come from Dr. Parmar or Dr. Zala, in person or on video.

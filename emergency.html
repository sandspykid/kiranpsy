Someone is talking about ending their life

Asking does not plant the idea. It tells the person you can hear it.

Do

  • Say: "I am here. Tell me what is happening." Then listen without interrupting.
  • Ask directly: "Are you thinking of killing yourself?" A plain question is safer than a hint.
  • Remove the means: medicines, pesticides, phenyl, ropes, knives, the keys to the terrace. Lock them away or hand them to a neighbour.
  • Stay with them, or make sure someone does. Do not leave them alone, even to sleep.
  • Call the clinic line or 14416 while you sit with them. Put the phone on speaker if that helps.
  • If they have already taken something, call 108 first.

Do not

  • Do not argue, lecture, or say "think of your family".
  • Do not promise to keep it secret.
  • Do not leave them alone to "cool down".
  • Do not give alcohol or a sleeping tablet to settle them.
  • Do not wait for morning to see if it passes.
There is no police case for the person Section 115 of the Mental Healthcare Act 2017 says a person who attempts suicide is presumed to be under severe stress and is not tried or punished. Hospitals know this. Fear of the police should never delay a call.

A relative is agitated, suspicious or not making sense

Often this is someone who stopped medicines some weeks ago. The aim tonight is safety, not a cure.

Do

  • Lower your voice. Slow down. Give them space and a clear path to the door.
  • One calm person talks. Everyone else leaves the room, including children.
  • Say what you are doing before you do it: "I am going to sit here."
  • Quietly remove sharp objects, hot pans, vehicle keys and the medicine box from reach.
  • Offer water or tea. Hunger and thirst make agitation worse.
  • Call the clinic line. Tell the doctor which medicines they take and when they stopped.

Do not

  • Do not argue with what they believe. You will not win, and it escalates.
  • Do not crowd, corner, grab or tie them.
  • Do not whisper to each other in front of them.
  • Do not hide medicine in food. It breaks trust for months.
  • Do not call a tantrik or wait for a "good day" to act.

When to call 108 or 112

  • They have a weapon, or have hurt someone or themselves.
  • They are trying to leave the house at night and cannot be kept safe.
  • They have not eaten or drunk for a day, or have a fever with the confusion.

Admission against a person's wishes is possible under the Mental Healthcare Act, but only with safeguards: a second doctor's opinion, the nominated representative's involvement, and review by the Board. The clinic doctor will tell you how it works. Read the rights the Act protects.

Someone is having a seizure

Most seizures stop on their own within two minutes. Your job is to keep them from being hurt and to watch the clock.

Do

  • Note the time it started. The length matters more than anything else.
  • Move furniture and hard objects away. Cushion the head with a folded dupatta or towel.
  • Loosen the collar, tie or dupatta around the neck. Remove spectacles.
  • Once the jerking stops, turn them onto their side so saliva drains out.
  • Stay until they are fully awake and know where they are. Speak calmly; they will be confused.
  • If you can, record a short video. It helps the doctor more than any description.

Do not

  • Do not put anything in the mouth. No spoon, no keys, no cloth, no fingers. The tongue cannot be swallowed.
  • Do not hold them down or try to stop the jerking.
  • Do not give water, food or medicine until they are fully awake.
  • Do not put a shoe or onion to the nose. It does nothing.
Call 108 if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, a second one starts before they wake, it happened in water, the person is pregnant or has diabetes, they are injured, they have trouble breathing afterwards, or this is their first ever seizure.

After any seizure, call the clinic in the morning. The EEG and epilepsy clinic can fit you in and the doctor will want to know whether a dose was missed.

Someone has taken too many tablets, or a poison

Every minute counts. This is a hospital emergency, not a clinic one.

Do

  • Call 108 now. Say what was taken, how much, and when, as best you know.
  • Keep the strips, bottle or packet, even if empty, and take it to the hospital.
  • Keep them awake and talking if you can. If they are unconscious, turn them on their side.
  • Note the time and what you see: vomiting, drowsiness, breathing, the colour of the lips.
  • Call the clinic line on the way so the doctor can speak to the hospital if needed.

Do not

  • Do not make them vomit. With some poisons and tablets, vomiting does more harm.
  • Do not give alcohol, coffee, milk or home remedies.
  • Do not wait to see how they feel in an hour.
  • Do not leave them alone in a bathroom or on a bed.

Coming to the clinic at night

  • Call first. The doctor on call tells you whether to come to the clinic on Jail Road or go straight to a hospital. Do not drive across town to a closed door.
  • Bring the medicine strips the person is taking, or was taking.
  • Bring the previous file or prescription, if there is one.
  • Bring one calm relative. Not the whole family.
